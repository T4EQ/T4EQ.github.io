Welcome back! In our intro post, we shared why we started this blog in the first place. Today, we want to share our very first real challenge! 

We began small, just a couple of us discussing our ideas over coffee, but pretty quickly we grew into a group of about nine like-minded people. We had the drive and the skills, but once we got together, the real questions hit us: *How do we actually do what we want to do? Who do we help? How do we actually reach out? How do we let people believe we can solve?*

When we looked around, we found a few platforms for traditional volunteering like manual labor, but nothing that allowed individuals to contribute through tech skills on a societal project close to communities or problem owners.

On top of that, though we did find some interesting calls aligned with our mission, civic engagement seemed largely tied to university affiliations or official institutions, excluding everyday citizens, independent developers, and local groups from directly participating. (*As readers, if you know of avenues differently from this, we are all ears!*)

We also saw hackathons aimed at UN SDG goals, but broad calls like "how do we improve hunger or poverty?" felt so abstract and distant. The intentions were good, but we felt lightyears away from real problem owners. All in all, finding our first project was definitely not easy.

But the fact was, we didn’t need to know all the answers right away, we just needed to start somewhere!

Through a friend of mine, Divya (Thank you!!), who had been involved with NGOs in Chennai, India, I got put in contact with Selva from AID India, an NGO working to improve living conditions, education, and shelter for the poor. This was our first exciting moment: a real contact to start with!

We scheduled our first call, introduced our mission, and they were thrilled to have us collaborate. Their founder, Balaji, opened up about a variety of challenges they had, and we decided to focus on the rural and remote education centers they were building, called the Eureka Education Centers.
They highlighted their biggest hurdles: ceiling lights for rural schools where electricity was poor and intermittent, and bad or non-existent internet in extremely remote areas.

It left us buzzing with questions: How do you provide education content in these places? How do you bridge this gap and make it slightly less unequal?
After the call, it stimulated so many thoughts and directions, but suddenly we had a whole tank of ideas to go to! By establishing our first community partnership with AID India, we had set our footing and officially started our path.

Still, big challenges remained before we could kick off our first project, [LEAP](/projects/). Our on-field partners weren't necessarily technical, so how do you translate real, non-tech struggles into technical requirements to co-define a solution together?

Stay tuned for our next post to see how we bridged that gap before starting LEAP officially!

*Are you working with a similar mission? Have you faced similar challenges? How did you tackle them? We'd love to hear from you in the comments below!*
